Why I built it

I was dealing with heart rate swings, high and low, that nobody seemed to take seriously. I wanted a quiet 24/7 way to record HR and HRV, then look back at the patterns myself over weeks and months.

I also stream and play games. I'd been using Pulsoid for the OBS widget and realized I wanted one app that did both. I wanted the stream widget and the long-term record from one strap, without a subscription.

Questions

Do I need an account?

No. Open monitor works without one, and sessions stay on this device until you export them. Once the page has loaded, recording keeps going without a connection.

Sign in with Google to save sessions to your own Drive and open them on any device signed in to the same account. Broadcasting and CSV export work either way.

Can I record all day?

The Android app keeps recording with the screen off or the phone locked. Browsers slow down background tabs. Keep the tab visible and the screen awake, or use the Android app.

Uses more battery than idle. Stop recording when you're done.

What does HRV measure?

RMSSD: how much the gap between beats changes. It moves with sleep, activity and time of day.

Is my data private?

CSVs stay on your device, or in your own Google Drive when signed in. No server stores your data. The broadcast relay passes live readings through to viewers and keeps nothing.

Can two people share one broadcast?

No. Each session has its own broadcast key. Anyone with it can watch; one device sends to it at a time.

Is this a medical device?

No. It shows heart rate and an HRV estimate for personal and creative use. It is not a diagnostic tool and does not replace clinical equipment or advice.
