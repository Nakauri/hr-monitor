The new aorti

October 12, 2026

Below are the main screens before and after, on the same sample data. Each one says what was wrong and what I did about it. Click any picture to see it full size; the arrow keys move between them.

The Day view

The Day view used to open on a 24-hour bar of coloured blocks with one card per recording. To see the graph you clicked "View as full-day timeline", and that page put a row of numbers and a panel of interpretation cards above the chart, so the graph started near the bottom of the screen.

Now the day opens straight on the graph, one click less. The summary numbers sit on one even grid above it, the whole day runs underneath with gaps left as gaps, and the interpretation cards moved below the chart.

The date in the title opens a calendar, so getting to any day is one tap instead of paging with the arrows or switching to Month first.

Zooming into the chart

Zooming was the worst part. Dragging the strip under the chart redrew the whole chart on every mouse move, so on my phone it stuttered and sometimes froze. A drag now paints at most once per frame, and the marks are drawn in one batched pass.

On a test that slows the processor six times to roughly phone speed, one redraw while panning went from about 125 ms to about 14 ms. Phones also get pinch and double-tap zoom, and on a laptop you can drag across the chart to zoom to that stretch.

The markers list used to sit beside the chart and squeeze it. It now opens from the Markers button above the chart, which shows the count, for example "Markers · 26". On a laptop the list opens over the chart; on the phone it opens as a sheet from the bottom. Tap a marker and the chart zooms to it.

Month

Month kept its calendar. The lens buttons above it were small all-caps pills, and on a laptop one of them wrapped onto two lines; they are now normal buttons that fit their words.

The arrows sit at the ends of the row and stay put however long the month name is, and the month name opens a picker for the year.

Laptop

Phone

Week

Week works as it did, one card per day with its trace. The header changed: it is the same date control as Day and Month, so the arrows are in the same place on all three and the dates open a calendar.

The view dropdown in the top bar became plain tabs on a laptop. On a phone it stays a dropdown, now full height and easy to hit.

Laptop

Phone

Trends

Trends was a fixed stack of charts with jargon under each title. Each chart is now a widget: the Widgets button turns them on and off and sets each one to half or full width, and you can drag them into the order you want.

The titles and the line under each one are in plain words now.

Phone

The monitor while recording

On the monitor, the big red Stop session button sat open near the top, next to the posture buttons. With the phone in a pocket that is easy to hit by accident.

Now the state reads as one line (Live, the strap and the session time), and Stop, posture, sharing and the log sit behind one Controls row that is closed every time the page opens. One tap opens it, and Stop still asks before it ends anything.

Laptop

Phone

Settings

Settings has the same tabs. Under them, every field, checkbox and button is now the same component the viewer uses, so a checkbox looks and behaves the same everywhere and stays readable when you hover or tick it.

Before, the monitor had its own toggle switch, its own buttons and labels in tiny capitals.

Laptop

Phone

The stream widget

The widget changed least, on purpose: it is the part your viewers see. Its labels are in the same Work Sans as the app, with units in lower case.

The switches for what it shows were there before. The home page now shows the four layouts most people want, with each one's footprint, so you can size the OBS source before you go live.

Faster where it was slow

Speed is hard to see in a still picture. These numbers come from tests with the processor slowed down to roughly phone speed.

  • Panning a zoomed day: about 125 ms per redraw before, about 14 ms now.
  • Clicking a marker to jump to it: the worst stall went from about 400 ms to about 55 ms.
  • Moving the mouse over the chart no longer redraws the chart at all; the hover line is drawn on its own.
  • Opening the viewer shows your saved sessions first, in well under a second, and syncs with Drive quietly after.
